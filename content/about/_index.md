## Background

I am an engineer trained in automation and control systems, with a foundation in signal processing, system modeling, and industrial-scale integration. My early training emphasized structured system thinking, reliability under operational constraints, and hardware–software co-design.

## Transition to Scientific Instrumentation

In 2025, I joined the IceCube Summer Student Program at the Wisconsin IceCube Particle Astrophysics Center (WIPAC), where I worked on detector instrumentation for IceCube-Gen2 optical modules (LOM-16). My work focused on waveform-level validation using cosmic-ray muons, bridging hardware characterization, calibration logic, and physics-facing observables.

This experience marked a transition from industrial automation to scientific instrumentation, expanding my focus from control systems to large-scale experimental physics infrastructure.

## Current Research Direction

My current research explores deadtime effects and trigger robustness in distributed optical arrays. I develop liveness-aware formulations and structure-preserving signal-processing concepts to mitigate efficiency loss under partial sensor non-availability.

I am particularly interested in scalable validation methodologies, real-time trigger architectures, and measurement robustness in extreme-environment deployments.
